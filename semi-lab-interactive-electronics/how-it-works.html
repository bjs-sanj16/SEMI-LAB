How It Works: 555 Astable & PWM Kinetics

A step-by-step technical breakdown of capacitor charge trajectories, comparator voltage thresholds, the SR flip-flop state machine, and the pulse-width modulation formulas governing the Semi Lab workbench.

The Astable PWM Equations

Deriving the timing intervals, oscillation frequency, and duty cycle when steering diodes isolate the charge and discharge paths.

Charge Time (T_high)

thigh = 0.693 · Ra · C1

Capacitor C1 charges from 13 Vcc up to 23 Vcc through potentiometer top segment Ra and forward-biased diode D1. During this phase, Pin 3 output is HIGH.

Discharge Time (T_low)

tlow = 0.693 · Rb · C1

Capacitor C1 discharges from 23 Vcc down to 13 Vcc through diode D2 and potentiometer bottom segment Rb into internal discharge transistor Pin 7. Pin 3 is LOW.

Total Frequency (f)

f = 1.44(Ra + Rb) · C1

Because Ra + Rb = Rtotal = 100 kΩ at every knob position, the sum of charging and discharging resistances remains constant, locking the frequency to 1.44 kHz!

Duty Cycle (D)

Duty = RaRa + Rb = RaRtotal

The duty cycle is simply the resistance ratio of the potentiometer wiper position, providing a smooth, continuous linear sweep from 1% to 99%.

Average DC Output (V_avg)

Vavg = Duty · Vsupply = RaRa + Rb · Vcc

The effective DC voltage seen by the load is the time-weighted average of full supply pulses: Vavg = 1T ∫0T v(t) dt = D × Vcc.

Conduction Power Loss (P_loss)

P = Iload2 · Rds(on) · Duty

Because the IRFZ44N MOSFET on-resistance Rds(on) is only 17.5 mΩ, the power lost as heat is negligible (a few milliwatts), achieving >95% electrical efficiency.

555 Timer Internal State Machine

Walk step-by-step through the internal sequence that generates continuous PWM oscillation.

Step 1 of 4

Capacitor Charging Phase

Step 1: Charging Ramp (Ton)
Current leaves the +Vcc rail, flows through upper potentiometer branch Ra, and passes forward-biased diode D1 into timing capacitor C1. The capacitor voltage starts ramping upwards from 1/3 Vcc (4.0V) toward 2/3 Vcc (8.0V). During this time, the internal SR latch is SET, the Pin 7 discharge transistor is OFF, and Pin 3 output is HIGH (+12V), driving the MOSFET gate ON.

Why the Semi Lab Circuit Excels

1. The Potentiometer + Dual-Diode Innovation

In a standard 555 astable oscillator without diodes, charging occurs through (Ra + Rb) and discharging occurs through Rb. This makes it impossible to achieve duty cycles below 50%, and changing the duty cycle involuntarily alters the oscillation frequency.

By adding opposed fast switching diodes D1 and D2, we split the potentiometer into two independent paths:

  • D1 steers current only during charging (through Ra).
  • D2 steers current only during discharging (through Rb).

Because Ra + Rb = 100 kΩ at all times, the total resistance sum never changes. As a result, the frequency stays locked at 1.44 kHz across the full 1% to 99% duty sweep!

2. The Flyback Diode & Inductive Kickback

Fans and electric motors store energy in their magnetic field (E = 12 L · I2). When the power MOSFET turns OFF abruptly in under 50 nanoseconds, the rate of current collapse (didt) is massive.

Faraday's Law of Induction dictates that the inductor generates an opposing voltage spike:

Vspike = -L · didt

Without protection, this kickback spike can reach 100V–200V, exceeding the IRFZ44N's 55V drain-to-source rating and destroying the semiconductor. Diode D3 forward-biases the instant the drain rises above Vcc + 0.7V, recirculating the stored magnetic current harmlessly through the motor coil until it dissipates.

Did you know? The NE555 timer IC was designed by legendary semiconductor engineer Hans Camenzind in 1971 for Signetics. It gets its name from the three matching 5 kΩ precision internal resistors that form the reference divider. Over 50 years later, billions of 555 timers are still manufactured every single year, making it the most successful integrated circuit in human history!
